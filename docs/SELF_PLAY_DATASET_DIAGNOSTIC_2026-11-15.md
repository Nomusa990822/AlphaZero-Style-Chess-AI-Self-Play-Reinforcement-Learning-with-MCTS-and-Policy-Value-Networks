## Scope and provenance

This is a read-only analysis of artifact [14176203642](https://github.com/Nomusa990822/AlphaZero-Style-Chess-AI-Self-Play-Reinforcement-Learning-with-MCTS-and-Policy-Value-Networks/actions/runs/43750799705/artifacts/14176203642), generated by workflow [43750799705](https://github.com/Nomusa990822/AlphaZero-Style-Chess-AI-Self-Play-Reinforcement-Learning-with-MCTS-and-Policy-Value-Networks/actions/runs/43750799705).

The two immutable checkpoint files passed their pinned SHA-256 checks. Both datasets were generated on CPU with the same declared settings: seed 20261115, 8 self-play games per checkpoint, 40 MCTS simulations, 120-ply cap, Dirichlet root noise enabled (alpha 0.5, epsilon 0.30), opening temperature 1.3 for the first 30 plies, then 0.5. No training, checkpoint mutation, or promotion was performed.

The audit report marked both datasets and the combined audit valid. The audit re-parsed every PGN, checked every move for legality, checked every recorded FEN and side-to-move label against replay, and checked every value target against the final result and mover perspective.

## Value-target and perspective checks

All 1,520 recorded position targets (793 baseline + 727 candidate) matched the PGN-reconstructed final outcome from the side-to-move perspective. No label mismatch was found.

- Baseline: 725 draw targets, 34 White-to-move losses, and 34 Black-to-move wins.
- Candidate by side-to-move: White had 27 losses, 280 draws, and 58 wins (365 positions); Black had 56 losses, 279 draws, and 27 wins (362 positions). Across both sides this totals 83 `-1`, 559 `0`, and 85 `+1` targets.
- Baseline mean target by side-to-move: White -0.08564; Black +0.08586.
- Candidate mean target by side-to-move: White +0.08493; Black -0.08011.

These means describe the outcome labels in these particular generated games. They are **not** neural-network value predictions, value calibration measurements, or estimates of playing strength. The JSONL records do not contain the model's raw value predictions, so calibration error cannot be computed from this artifact.

## Independence and pairing limitation

The identical seed and search settings do not make these games paired. The checkpoints produce different policy distributions, which leads to different sampled moves and different trajectories. The first two plies differ in every corresponding baseline/candidate game number. Across the full datasets there are only three shared normalized board states (FEN board, turn, castling and en-passant fields), so this is not a matched-position comparison.

The observed difference in decisive-game frequency (1/8 versus 3/8) is therefore descriptive only. It does not show that the candidate is stronger, more accurate, or better calibrated. Eight stochastic games per checkpoint are far too few for a reliable strength estimate, and the move-cap draws further limit outcome information.

## Data-quality observations

1. **Position count convention:** one sample is stored for each pre-move position. Therefore positions equal plies played; the terminal post-game board is not an additional training sample.
2. **Move-cap flags:** move-cap games carry both `ended_by_move_limit=true` and `ended_by_adjudication=true`. In this implementation, “adjudication” means the configured neutral draw at the move cap; it is not a Stockfish or material-based adjudication.
3. **Elapsed-time field:** every JSONL record has `elapsed_seconds=0.0`. The dataset script currently passes the literal zero to `MetricsLogger`, rather than the per-game duration already exposed by `SelfPlayGenerator.generate_games(on_game_complete=...)`. This does not affect moves, outcomes, FENs, or value targets, but per-game timing in this artifact is unusable. Correct the instrumentation in a separate small code change before relying on timing metrics.
4. **Opening diversity:** first moves vary, but these are model-sampled openings, not a fixed opening suite. Corresponding game numbers do not share opening positions.

## Conclusions and next evaluation

**Validated:** the immutable checkpoints can be loaded for inference; both datasets preserve complete PGNs and per-ply position/value records; the audit confirms move legality, replayed FENs, side-to-move perspective, and final-outcome target consistency.

**Not established:** relative playing strength, Elo, tactical accuracy, strategic quality, value calibration, or training readiness.

Before any training or promotion decision, design a separate paired held-out evaluation: use a fixed opening suite, play each opening with colours swapped, pit baseline directly against candidate under identical search budgets, record complete game-level outcomes, and use a predeclared sample size and statistical promotion criterion. Keep value calibration as a separate held-out analysis requiring model predictions paired with game-level outcome labels.
